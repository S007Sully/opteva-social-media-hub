Med Spa Instagram Post Ideas From 26 Years of Running One

Post ideas organized by what they do for the business: first bookings, rebookings, easier consults, slow Tuesdays. Plus the five I stopped doing.

Most med spa feeds look the same because most of them were built from the same lists. Treatment of the week. Before-and-after. Team photo. Quote graphic. Repeat.

I ran a med spa for 26 years. For most of those years I was the one deciding what went on Instagram, and for the last several I was the one watching which posts actually turned into a booked appointment. This is the list I wish someone had handed me, organized the only way that ever mattered: by what the post is supposed to do for the business.

A post that gets a like and a post that gets a booking are two different posts. Most lists do not tell you which is which.

Posts that get a first booking

These are for the person who has been following you for three months and has not called. They are not short on interest. They are short on a reason to go first.

  • The "what actually happens" walkthrough. Thirty seconds, phone camera, one treatment, from check-in to leaving. No music over the talking. The number one reason people do not book is that they cannot picture the appointment.
  • The question you have answered a hundred times. Write it as the caption, answer it in one paragraph. "Does it hurt" and "how long until I see something" outperformed every polished graphic we ever paid for.
  • The price, said out loud. Not a menu. One treatment, one price, what it includes. The people who scroll past because of price were never going to book; the people who were waiting to hear it will.
  • The consult-only post. "You can come in and just ask questions. Nobody will sell you anything." Say it plainly once a month. It books more than any discount did.

Posts that get a rebooking

Your existing clients are the cheapest bookings you will ever get, and almost nobody posts for them.

  • The treatment-cycle reminder. If a result lasts twelve weeks, post about it in week ten. Not a promo, just "this is around the time you'll start noticing."
  • The "you asked, we added it" post. New service, framed as a response to the people already coming in.
  • Membership math, done honestly. What a regular actually spends in a year with and without the membership. Real numbers.

Posts that fill a slow day without discounting

Discounting trains your clients to wait for the discount. These do not.
  1. Same-week openings, posted Monday. "Thursday afternoon has two spots." Specific beats general every time.
  2. The staff pick. One provider, one treatment they would get themselves, why.
  3. The quiet-hours post. Some clients will pay to come in when nobody else is there. Tell them when that is.

Five post types I stopped doing

  • Quote graphics. Zero bookings in a decade.
  • Before-and-afters without the consent workflow behind them. The risk is not worth the likes, and there is a right way to do them.
  • "Happy Friday" posts. Nobody needed us to tell them.
  • Giveaways. They bring followers who want free things.
  • Anything I would not say to a client's face in the room.

How often to post

Three times a week, every week, beats seven times one week and none the next. Consistency is what the algorithm and your clients are both reading. If you can only manage two, manage two.

FAQ

How many posts a week does a med spa need?

Two to three, consistently. The feeds that grew for us were never the busiest ones; they were the ones that never went quiet.

Do I need Reels, or are photos enough?

Reels reach people who do not follow you yet. Photos serve the people who do. You need both, but if you are starting from nothing, start with one walkthrough Reel a week.

What should a new med spa post before it has any results to show?

Your space, your training, your answers to common questions, and your consult-only offer. Results come later; trust can start now.
